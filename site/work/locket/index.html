Locket: home insurance, from a B2C product to a B2B platform

Founding engineer and Head of Software Engineering 2020–2021 · CTO 2021–2024

Context

Locket is a UK home insurance company, regulated by the FCA. It sold policies directly and through price comparison sites. I built the first B2C product. Later, as CTO, I led the change to a B2B model and built a new platform for other insurers.

  • ~2 minto buy a policy (other insurers: 15 min or more)
  • 1–2 sper price
  • 30M+ rowsUK addresses loaded in ~1 min
  • ~15people in the team

The B2C product

  • Pricing engine. I wrote it in Elixir. It is a pipeline of pure functions, one step for each rating factor. It calls the data providers in parallel and returns a price in 1 to 2 seconds. When the customer changes the cover, the price changes at once.
  • Fewer questions. External data providers and our own data APIs replaced most of the questions on the form.
  • Address search. A UK address search that loads more than 30 million address rows in approximately 1 minute with streamed SQL.
  • Policy changes at any time. Customers could change their policy as often as they wanted, with immediate pro-rated payments and refunds.
  • Events and reports. A transactional event table with the money and compliance data for each change. From it, the system made the daily and monthly bordereaux for each underwriter. Each underwriter had different requirements.
  • Market data. I collected competitor prices from price comparison sites with Python and Selenium. This data was the base of the pricing model. I also set up the AWS resources for the data scientist to train and serve the model.

Elixir / Phoenix, Ruby on Rails, PostgreSQL, Kong, Heroku, AWS S3, Angular, Flutter

The B2B platform

A multi-tenant, API-first insurance platform with a Flutter SDK. As CTO I was also the main engineer of this platform. I wrote the infrastructure, the services, the SDK and the product, and the team built with me. Large insurers did security reviews of it.

  • Compute. EKS. Karpenter on Fargate provisioned Spot nodes where possible. Replicas across availability zones, with horizontal autoscaling.
  • Delivery. Terraform with one Terraform Cloud workspace for each environment. ArgoCD GitOps as an app of apps, with a Kustomize base and overlays. Two clusters: development and staging, and production.
  • Services. Node.js and TypeScript, event-driven, with gRPC between services and Kafka for events.
  • Gateway. Kong checked a JWT for each tenant, with a different salt for each tenant.
  • Data. RDS PostgreSQL in high availability with a read replica. Each B2B customer had its own database.
  • Security. Encryption in transit and at rest, AWS Secrets Manager, a Bugcrowd penetration test program and phishing simulations for all staff.
  • Recovery. Backups of everything, and automated restore tests.
  • Install. A customer could install the platform into its own AWS account in one step, with Terraform, ArgoCD and Kustomize.

Node.js / TypeScript, gRPC, Kafka, PostgreSQL (RDS), Kong, EKS, Karpenter, Terraform, ArgoCD, AWS Secrets Manager, CloudWatch, Kubecost, Flutter

Team

  • Approximately 15 people in London and Łódź: 8 engineers, 2 data scientists, 3 designers, 1 QA engineer and 1 project manager.
  • I hired people, did code reviews and set the git process.
  • I started an on-call rotation with runbooks, scripts, a knowledge base and incident drills.
  • I presented the technology to investors and to the IT departments of insurers. I worked with the CEO, the CFO and the Chief Insurance Officer.

Company results

  • The company raised more than £1 million on Seedrs.
  • It won Home Insurance at the 2023 National Insurance Awards.
  • Thousands of customers paid for policies.
