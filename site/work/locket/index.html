From the first line of code to CTO of an insurer

Locket is a UK home insurer regulated by the FCA. It sold policies directly and through price comparison sites. I built the first B2C product. As CTO I led the move to a B2B model and built a platform that other insurers ran on.

The B2C product

A customer could buy a policy in about 2 minutes. Other insurers needed 15 or more.

  • Pricing engine. I wrote it in Elixir as a pipeline of pure functions, one step for each rating factor. It calls the data providers in parallel and returns a price in 1 to 2 seconds. When the customer changes the cover, the price changes at once.
  • Fewer questions. External data providers and our own data APIs replaced most of the questions on the form.
  • Address search. A UK address search that loads more than 30 million address rows in about 1 minute with streamed SQL.
  • Policy changes at any time. Customers could change their policy as often as they wanted, with immediate pro-rated payments and refunds.
  • Events and reports. A transactional event table with the money and compliance data for each change. From it, the system made the daily and monthly bordereaux for each underwriter, each with its own requirements.
  • Market data. I collected competitor prices from price comparison sites with Python and Selenium. This data was the base of the pricing model.

Elixir / Phoenix · Ruby on Rails · PostgreSQL · Kong · Heroku · AWS S3 · Angular · Flutter

The B2B platform

A multi-tenant, API-first insurance platform with a Flutter SDK. As CTO I was also its main engineer: I wrote the infrastructure, the services, the SDK and the product, and the team built with me. Large insurers did security reviews of it.

  • Compute. EKS. Karpenter provisioned Spot nodes where possible. Replicas across availability zones, with horizontal autoscaling.
  • Delivery. Terraform with one workspace for each environment. ArgoCD GitOps as an app of apps, with a Kustomize base and overlays.
  • Services. Node.js and TypeScript, event-driven, with gRPC between services and Kafka for events.
  • Gateway. Kong checked a JWT for each tenant, with a different salt for each tenant.
  • Data. RDS PostgreSQL in high availability with a read replica. Each B2B customer had its own database.
  • Security. Encryption in transit and at rest, AWS Secrets Manager, a Bugcrowd penetration test program and phishing simulations for all staff.
  • Recovery. Backups of everything, and automated restore tests.
  • Install. A customer could install the platform into its own AWS account in one step, with Terraform, ArgoCD and Kustomize.

Node.js / TypeScript · gRPC · Kafka · PostgreSQL (RDS) · Kong · EKS · Karpenter · Terraform · ArgoCD · Flutter
