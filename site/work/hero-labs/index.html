Hero Labs: an IoT platform for a smart water valve

Founding engineer, then Head of Engineering 2018–2020 · Interim CTO 2023–2024

Context

Hero Labs (now Watergate) makes Sonic, a smart water valve for homes. The founder and CEO asked me to design and build the platform from the start. Later I ran engineering. In 2023 the company asked me back as interim CTO.

The original Sonic ran on batteries, with no Wi-Fi or mains power at the valve. It talked to a hub over a custom radio protocol, and the hub connected to the IoT platform. Each device sent telemetry every second: water flow, internal and external temperature and water pressure.

  • 1 stelemetry interval for each device
  • £1m in 3 monthsraised by the company with the first product version

The platform

  • AWS IoT over MQTT, with a broker fleet, device provisioning, over-the-air updates and telemetry at high event rates.
  • I built the cloud infrastructure, the backend and the mobile app.
  • The company got its first funding with a prototype that I built with one other engineer and an embedded engineer.

AWS (IoT Core, Lambda, DynamoDB, Kinesis), Terraform, Elixir, Ruby on Rails, Python

Appliance recognition

Each use of water (a tap, a shower, a washing machine) has its own pattern of flow and pressure. The goal was a model that finds the appliance for each water event. With it, the product can detect anomalies and leaks.

  • The first training data came from lab tests. In the alpha and beta tests in real homes, this data was not sufficient.
  • The solution was a beta program where users labelled their own water events in the app. These labels became training data.
  • I built the full loop: the data lake and the ingestion pipelines for the data scientists, the labelling flow in the app, and the path from new labels to a new model release.
  • The model then assigned events to the correct appliance and detected anomalies and leaks.

Team

I ran the engineering department: 2 data scientists, software engineers, a hardware engineer, lab engineers and an industrial designer.

Interim CTO, 2023–2024

  • I moved the workloads from ECS to EKS, with Terraform, ArgoCD GitOps and Kubernetes plugins.
  • I rightsized the workloads on EKS and added S3 lifecycle rules that move old telemetry to deep archive. The cost decreased and the platform scaled better.
  • CloudWatch metrics and alerts, and Prometheus and Grafana for cost and performance.
  • I started DevOps practices, did a security audit and prepared the platform for external due diligence.
