# What top engineer and CTO sites do (October 2026)

Sites studied: lethain.com (Will Larson), stephango.com (Steph Ango), nat.org (Nat Friedman), brittanychiang.com, leerob.com, levels.io (Pieter Levels), antfu.me (Anthony Fu), rauno.me (Rauno Freiberg), plus the usual "best developer portfolio" roundups.

What the best ones have in common:

1. One sentence that says who you are and what you do, in your own voice. ("I build accessible, pixel-perfect experiences for the web." "I build internet startups by myself.") Here: the LinkedIn banner line, "I scrape the web at scale, build software products and cut cloud bills."
2. A point of view. Nat Friedman lists things he believes; Rauno opens with a manifesto. Here: "How I work", six habits, each tied to the system and the number that taught it.
3. Proof of work over claims: shipped things with numbers and links. levels.io and antfu.me lead with the products themselves. Here: results grid, product cards with real screenshots, case studies.
4. Stories with problem, decision and outcome instead of bullet lists. Here: three company stories with outcome visuals. Employer internals stay out.
5. A "now" section (Derek Sivers' /now convention, used by Steph Ango). Here: "Now", dated.
6. Writing as evidence of thinking (Larson, Ango, Robinson). Here: a small "Writing" block that points to LinkedIn posts. Next step: move 2 or 3 posts onto the site as notes.
7. Restraint plus one signature detail. Rauno and Brittany Chiang win on craft, not effects. Here: the animated Cartpie demo in the hero (URL in, page scanned, JSON out); everything else is quiet.
8. Fast and private: self-hosted fonts, no trackers, works without JS, dark mode, readable on a phone.
9. A clear way in: one email address, two routes (full-time or fractional), CV as a download, not as the page.
